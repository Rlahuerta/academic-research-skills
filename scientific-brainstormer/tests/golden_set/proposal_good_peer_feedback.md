## 1. Title & Abstract

**Title:** Investigating the Effect of Structured Peer Feedback on Undergraduate Physics Problem-Solving Accuracy

**Abstract:** Background: Peer feedback is widely used in STEM education but its impact on quantitative problem-solving accuracy remains understudied. Gap: Existing studies focus on essay writing; few isolate feedback structure as an independent variable. Objective: Compare structured peer feedback (rubric-guided) against unstructured peer feedback on problem-solving accuracy in introductory physics. Method: Randomized controlled trial with 120 undergraduates, pre/post Force Concept Inventory, and blinded grading of weekly problem sets. Anticipated Results: Structured feedback group will show a 15-20% greater improvement in accuracy scores. Implications: Provides evidence for rubric design in STEM peer assessment.

## 2. Background and Targeted Gap

Peer assessment literature (Topping, 1998; Liu & Carless, 2006) establishes that peer feedback improves metacognition, but findings on accuracy gains are mixed. Most studies examine essay-based disciplines. The gap is whether feedback structure (rubric-guided vs. free-form) affects quantitative problem-solving outcomes specifically in physics, where solutions have objectively correct answers.

## 3. Methodology & Experimental Design

* **Hypothesis:** If peer feedback is structured using a domain-specific rubric, then undergraduate physics problem-solving accuracy will improve more than with unstructured feedback, because the rubric directs reviewers to specific solution steps and common error types.

* **Control vs. Experimental Groups:**
  - Control: Unstructured peer feedback (reviewers receive only "provide feedback on the solution")
  - Experimental: Structured peer feedback (reviewers receive a physics problem-solving rubric with dimensions: conceptual approach, mathematical execution, unit consistency, assumption justification)
  - Both groups: n=60, matched on pre-test FCI scores, block-randomized

* **Data Collection Pipeline:**
  - Pre-test: Force Concept Inventory (FCI) — week 0
  - Weekly problem sets: 3 problems/week × 10 weeks = 30 problems per student
  - Peer feedback: submitted via LMS, tracked for word count and rubric dimension coverage
  - Post-test: FCI — week 11
  - Blinded grading: two physics TAs grade all problem sets independently; inter-rater reliability target κ ≥ 0.80

* **Statistical Analysis Plan:**
  - Primary outcome: ANCOVA on post-test problem-solving scores with pre-test FCI as covariate
  - Secondary: Mixed-effects model for weekly problem set scores (fixed: group, time; random: student)
  - Effect size: Cohen's d with 95% CI
  - Power analysis: n=120 provides 80% power to detect d=0.50 at α=0.05

## 4. Expected Limitations & Risk Mitigation

* **Limitation:** Single-institution sample limits generalizability.
  - Mitigation: Document institutional context thoroughly; provide all materials as supplementary for replication.
* **Limitation:** Rubric design may inadvertently favor certain solution styles.
  - Mitigation: Pilot rubric with 3 physics faculty and iterate before main study.
* **Limitation:** Peer feedback quality varies with reviewer ability.
  - Mitigation: Measure and report reviewer FCI scores as a covariate in secondary analysis.
