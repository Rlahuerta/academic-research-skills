# Golden Evaluation Set: Correlation-Causation Leap (Expected: FAIL)

## 1. Title & Abstract

**Title:** The Effect of Library Usage on Academic Performance

**Abstract:** We analyzed library entry swipe data and found that students who enter the library more than 3 times per week have GPAs 0.5 points higher than students who enter less than once per week. This demonstrates that library usage causes higher academic performance. We propose a study to confirm this causal relationship.

## 2. Background and Targeted Gap

Prior studies show correlation between library usage and GPA. This study aims to establish causality.

## 3. Methodology & Experimental Design

* **Hypothesis:** If students are given mandatory library hours, then their GPA will increase by 0.5 points because library access directly improves study quality.

* **Control vs. Experimental Groups:**
  - Experimental: Mandatory 10 hours/week in library
  - Control: Normal access (no mandate)

* **Data Collection Pipeline:** Collect GPA at end of semester.

* **Statistical Analysis Plan:** T-test comparing mean GPA between groups.

## 4. Expected Limitations & Risk Mitigation

The main limitation is that students who already use the library frequently may differ systematically (e.g., higher motivation, better time management). These confounding variables are not controlled for. Additionally, the mandatory intervention does not establish a causal mechanism — it only shows whether forced library time affects GPA.
